Positive Compliments at School: A Guide to Valid

By the Valid team · Published September 13, 2026

Valid — Compliment Classmates is built around a simple idea: give students a way to notice and appreciate the people around them. Positive polls invite classmates to recognize one another, while chats and Stories provide other ways to stay connected. The goal is a school community where kindness is easier to express.

What a meaningful compliment looks like

A thoughtful compliment recognizes something someone would feel comfortable being appreciated for: helping another student, bringing humor to a difficult day, or making a newcomer feel welcome. Those are examples of positive participation, not a promise that any particular prompt is available in the app.

Before answering a poll, consider how the person receiving the vote would read it. Choose sincerely. An inside joke that embarrasses someone, a sarcastic vote, or a coordinated attempt to single someone out can undermine a positive question. Our community rules prohibit bullying, harassment, and manipulating votes.

How Valid supports positive participation

Students answer polls about people they know, see compliments they receive, and earn aura. Valid’s published moderation policy is that every poll is reviewed by a human moderator before publication. Students also have reporting and blocking tools when an interaction needs attention.

These features support our aim of making appreciation part of everyday school life. They do not make a vote count a measure of someone’s character or popularity, and they do not mean every interaction will feel positive. A quieter student deserves the same respect as someone receiving many votes.

Keep participation comfortable

Give classmates room to choose whether they want to participate. Avoid asking someone to join repeatedly or pressuring them to reveal whom they voted for. If checking votes starts to feel stressful, taking a break or turning off notifications can help put the app back in perspective.

Families can start with a concrete conversation: Which interaction felt kind? Did anything feel awkward? Is there someone you would rather not interact with? Listening to those answers is more useful than treating the number of compliments as a score for how school is going.

Understand what anonymous means

Voter identities are hidden by default, but Valid does not offer complete anonymity. Reveal features can show a voter’s name and profile photo to the recipient. Give compliments you would be comfortable standing behind, and review purchase options together before paying for reveal benefits.

Valid is for students ages 13 and older. Read our parent FAQ for details about participation, identity reveals, and safety tools, and our community guidelines for the rules that apply to everyone.
