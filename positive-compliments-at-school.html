Thoughtful Compliments at School: Practical Examples

By the Valid team · Updated September 13, 2026

A thoughtful compliment notices something specific: welcoming a new classmate, helping a teammate, or making someone feel included. Valid’s positive polls are one way to recognize people around you. These examples also work in everyday school life.

Kindness: notice the small action

“You noticed I was having a rough day and checked in.” A concrete action is more personal than a broad ranking of who is nicest. In a poll, choose someone because the question genuinely reminds you of them.

Inclusion: appreciate who makes room

“You made sure the new person had someone to sit with.” Recognizing inclusion draws attention to a behavior others can practice, without turning the recipient into a popularity score.

Effort: recognize persistence

“You kept helping our group when the project got difficult.” Effort and reliability can be meaningful to notice even when someone is not the loudest person in the room.

Humor: make sure everyone is in on the joke

“You helped us laugh when the rehearsal got stressful.” Humor works as appreciation when the recipient feels included. A sarcastic vote or an inside joke meant to embarrass someone undermines a positive prompt.

Teamwork: name the contribution

“You listened to everyone’s ideas and helped us make a plan.” A sincere compliment can recognize listening, patience, creativity, or practical help rather than appearance or status.

These are illustrative examples, not a promise that a particular question is currently available in Valid.

How to use the same care in polls

  • Read the prompt and vote sincerely.
  • Consider how the recipient would interpret the vote.
  • Avoid organizing votes to single someone out or manipulate the result.
  • Respect someone who does not want to join or participate.
  • Do not pressure someone to say whom they voted for.

Valid’s published policy is human review of polls before publication. Reporting and blocking tools help when an interaction needs attention. No review process can know every private joke or conflict, so let us know if a positive-looking prompt is being used to hurt someone.

Give compliments you can stand behind

Voter identities are hidden by default, but reveal features can show a voter’s name and profile photo to a recipient. Valid does not offer complete anonymity. Kind participation should not depend on being impossible to identify.

Keep votes in perspective

A vote count is not a measure of someone’s value or the quality of their friendships. If checking the app starts to feel stressful, take a break, turn off notifications, or talk with someone you trust. Families can ask which interactions felt kind and whether anything felt uncomfortable.
