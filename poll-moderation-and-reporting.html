How Valid Moderates Polls and Handles Reports

By the Valid team · Published September 13, 2026

Positive prompts are central to Valid. Our published policy is that every poll is reviewed by a human moderator before it is published. That review is one part of a broader approach that includes community rules, student reporting, blocking, and support for families.

Human review before a poll is published

Reviewing a poll before publication creates an opportunity to identify a problem before students interact with it. Valid’s rules prohibit bullying, harassment, impersonation, and vote manipulation. Positive wording matters, and so does the possibility that a question could be used to embarrass or target someone.

A poll review is not a guarantee about everything that happens afterward. Sarcasm, private context, or conflict carried over from school may not be apparent from the question alone. A student’s report can supply information that a moderator did not have during the initial review.

What our moderation commitment means

Valid’s published moderation SLA is under 15 minutes: our commitment to a fast moderation turnaround when content needs review. This is a service commitment, not a published measurement showing that every report has been resolved within that time. Some situations require more context or follow-up.

The pre-publication review policy applies to polls. It should not be read as a statement that every chat message or Story receives the same review before anyone can see it. Families should use the reporting and blocking tools when any part of the experience feels wrong.

How to report something useful

Use the report control available on the relevant content or profile. If you contact support, describe where the interaction happened, the username involved, and what made it concerning. Explain any school context or repeated behavior that would not be obvious from the content alone.

Share screenshots and identifying details privately with support@validapp.lol. Avoid reposting an upsetting interaction publicly, where it can draw more attention to the student involved. You do not need to have a perfect explanation before asking for help.

Reporting and blocking serve different purposes

A report asks us to review a concern. Blocking is a tool students can use to help protect their own experience. Where both controls are available, a student can use both rather than continuing an uncomfortable interaction while waiting for a response.

We want students and parents to tell us when an interaction falls short of the experience we are trying to create. Read the community guidelines for our standards, the parent FAQ for wider safety information, or contact support for help with a specific situation.
